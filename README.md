Autonomous AI analysts can run hundreds of specifications in minutes and report the most striking, and recent studies show that they can be steered into specification search and that their conclusions track the priors they are given. We ask what an independent referee can promise about the findings of an analyst whose behaviour it cannot model. We propose the rolling seal: data are split into an open exploration set and sealed folds; the analyst explores freely and commits claims, each of whose required evidence is fixed at commit by e-LOND; at the end of each episode one fresh fold is opened once, each open claim’s betting e-value on it multiplies into the claim’s running evidence, and the spent fold is released to the analyst. We prove that the false discovery rate over certified claims is at most α for every analyst, every ordering and revision of claims and every dependence between them, that the guarantee rests on sealing rather than logging, and that commit-time choices by a language model, such as grouping tests into hypothesis families, cannot weaken it. We give exact nonparametric e-values for differences and levels of bounded means, with starting bets from the exploration data, and describe HERDSMAN, an open-source referee with an encrypted vault, a hash-chained ledger, signed certificates and a Model Context Protocol interface for agents. In 1,500 simulated studies, unrefereed reports contained false findings in 93% to 100% of null studies while the rolling seal’s false discovery rate never exceeded 1.0%; a re-used holdout that released per-claim estimates certified a composite claim built from noise in 100% of null studies, the rolling seal in 0%. Validity costs power, which we quantify. In a live case study, six instances of a frontier AI agent analysed a synthetic lending experiment under neutral, pressured, sceptical and segment-hunting briefs. Their unrefereed reports contained no false effects; every claim they then committed was true, and the referee certified 81 of the 101, leaving mainly small arrears effects unconfirmed.
